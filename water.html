Coursework, a research assistantship and two things I built because the data was sitting there. Every result below comes from a held-out test set, not the split I tuned on.

Bus arrival forecasting from live GPS feeds

Ingested a year of city transit pings, rebuilt each trip's progress along its route, and predicted arrival time at the next four stops. Gradient boosting on trip-so-far features beat the agency's static timetable, especially in the evening peak.

Mean absolute error 2.4 min, down from 5.1

Coral bleaching detection in satellite tiles

Fine-tuned a small ResNet on Sentinel-2 reef imagery with a reviewed label set of 3,800 tiles. Cloud and sun-glint tiles were the main failure mode, so I added a masking step before inference and reported per-reef accuracy rather than a single global score.

F1 0.87 on unseen reefs

Campus energy anomaly monitor

Half-hourly meter readings from 14 buildings, decomposed into seasonal and residual components, with residuals scored for outliers. Facilities now get a Monday digest; the first month surfaced a chiller running through two semester breaks.

31 flagged events, 24 confirmed real

Search across four semesters of lecture notes

A weekend project that stuck. Notes and slide text are chunked, embedded and served through a small local API, so I can ask a question in plain language and get the three passages that answer it. Evaluated against 120 questions I wrote myself.

About

I'm a final-year data science student. Most of what I enjoy sits before the model: finding out how a dataset was actually collected, what the missing rows mean, and whether the thing being measured is the thing anyone cares about.

Last summer I worked as a research assistant on the reef imagery project, which meant a lot of time defending label decisions to marine biologists. It made me much more careful about what a metric is really claiming.

Outside coursework I maintain the lecture-notes search tool for my cohort and help run the university's data society workshops on reproducible analysis.
